# Supplement to: Observational constraints on eddy saturation in the Antarctic Circumpolar Current

Cristina Martí-Solana, Simón Ruiz, Vincent Combes, Bàrbara Barceló-Llull, Maxime Ballarotta and Ananda Pascual

**Conventions.** Products follow main-text Table 1 (Product 1: all-satellite, 1/8°; Product 4: two-satellite, 1/4°). Unless stated, trends are Theil–Sen slopes of deseasonalised monthly series, 2001–2025, in percent of the period mean per decade, with 95 % intervals in brackets and p from Sect. S4; contrasts are trends of differences of mean-normalised series, in percentage points (points) per decade. Each table's CSV file lists the source of every number.

## S1 Altimetry product and constellation sensitivity

This section supports the main-text statements on the constellation dependence of the EKE trend, the sector and regional comparisons and the Drake Passage domains (Sects. 2.1, 3.1, 3.2 and 4).

**Method.** The record starts in 2001, with at least three altimeters continuously available. Effective resolution is coarser than the grid, of order 100–200 km in wavelength at ACC latitudes (Ballarotta et al., 2019, 2025), so EKE excludes scales near the first baroclinic deformation radius (10–20 km at 50–60° S, less farther south; Chelton et al., 1998). Area means are cosine-weighted and drop 1/8° pixels whose ice-flag fraction (fraction of days with the altimetry ice flag) exceeds 0.10 in any month; the half-power tracks, 1° Argo and wind cells and map shading use the product's permanent ice mask (98 % agreement); the Drake bins require pixels ice-free in every month. Product 4 is processed exactly as Product 1. EKE (main-text Eq. 1) is split exactly into a low-frequency part, ½|U − Ū|² of the monthly-mean velocity U about the 2001–2025 mean Ū, and a sub-monthly part, half the within-month velocity variance; the along-stream speed is U · Ū/|Ū| (Fig. S1b, c). Because anomalies are taken about a fixed mean, slowly varying anomalies enter both KE (½|U|²) and EKE, so the two overlap and do not partition the total energy. Sectors are the Atlantic (70° W–20° E), Indian (20–147° E) and Pacific (147° E–70° W) parts of the band.

**Results.** All band and sector trends have the same sign in both products (Table S1, Fig. S1). KE trends are similar; the Product 4 EKE trend is about half that of Product 1, the series diverging mainly after about 2018 (Fig. S1a). The difference (+4.3 [−2.2, +10.8] points per decade, p = 0.12) is not significant, which does not establish equivalence. The constellation dependence sits mainly in the sub-monthly part (+12.0 vs +5.0 % per decade), less in the low-frequency part (+5.6 vs +3.2). The along-stream speed increases by +2.0 [1.4, 2.5] and +1.8 [1.2, 2.4] % per decade (p = 0.003 in both), a significant, nearly product-independent surface acceleration. Pacific EKE is significant in both products, Indian EKE in Product 1 only, Atlantic in neither. Hogg et al. (2015) found the weakest increase in the Atlantic; here the Atlantic trend is least significant but similar in relative terms. Zhang et al. (2021, crossover data since 1993) found significant increase only downstream of the Campbell Plateau. In 2° bins (Fig. 3d) the maxima near the Macquarie Ridge (+103 cm² s⁻² per decade) lie at 153–155° E, west of the ridge crest; the largest negative bins, −26 to −30 at 27–31° E over the Southwest Indian Ridge, are not significant. The relative EKE trend of four pre-specified boxes ("downstream boxes") downstream of the Kerguelen Plateau (75–95° E), the Southeast Indian Ridge–Macquarie–Campbell region (145–175° E), the Pacific–Antarctic Ridge (150–125° W) and Drake Passage (55–35° W) (23 % of band area, 42–43 % of mean EKE) does not differ from that of the rest of the band (Table S1). In the regions of earlier studies, Product 1 EKE trends for 2001–2025 (Hogg et al., 2015, Indian, Pacific and Atlantic boxes: +9.0, +8.6, +8.8 % per decade; 35–65° S box, a proxy for the domain of Martínez-Moreno et al., 2021: +6.6) have the same sign as, and overlap within intervals with, the published estimates for earlier periods (Fig. S1d) [AUTHOR CHECK: Hogg et al. (2015) values and intervals in Fig. S1d]. Over 2001–2012 (overlap with Hogg et al., 2015) Product 1 box trends are +5.8 % (Indian) and +0.4 % (Pacific), neither significant, so the agreement reflects post-2012 change.

**Drake Passage domains.** The band between the mean SAF and PF is bounded by the central streamlines of the SAF and PF zones of Gutierrez-Villanueva et al. (2023), ψ = −0.5 and −1.125 m, mapped onto the Product 1 mean ADT with ADT = 0.62 + 1.04ψ (r = 0.99, 4005 streamline pairs on the transects): +0.105 and −0.542 m. Pixels are ice-free in every month. EKE is averaged (cos-latitude weights) in 2° longitude bins (Fig. 2c), grouped into upstream (86–68° W), passage (68–58° W) and downstream (58–40° W) domains. The 2016–2025 mean exceeds the 2001–2015 mean by 23.9 %, 21.2 % and 11.6 %, respectively (mean of bin means; Fig. S2a, b). Bins with uncorrected p < 0.05 lie upstream (85, 83, 77° W) and in the Scotia Sea (53, 51, 47° W), none in the passage. Half-power analyses use 70–55° W (Sect. S3).

## S2 Drake Passage section

This section supports the main-text statements on the agreement between altimetric and shipboard velocities (Sect. 2.3) and on the trend of the surface integral Qₛ (Sects. 3.1 and 4).

**Comparison with the ADCP record.** The ADCP record (Gutierrez-Villanueva et al., 2023) provides cross-track velocity at 70–118 m, transport over 0–760 m and EKE integrated over 0–780 m (the archived variable); same-period comparisons use its 237 transects of October 2005–April 2019. Altimetric velocity from each transect's monthly field is averaged in 25 km bins along the mean track. Product 1 reproduces the mean cross-track velocity profile (r = 0.98; Fig. S4a) and the velocity per bin and transect (r = 0.72, 8187 pairs; 0.61 with bin means removed; RMS difference 15 cm s⁻¹; Product 4 within 0.02 in r). The 2005–2019 velocity-trend profiles in distance bins correlate with r = 0.57 (Product 1; 0.63 Product 4), and the 2001–2025 altimetric profile with the in situ one with r = 0.64 (Fig. S3). Trends cannot be validated over 15 years.

**Coordinate systems.** Gutierrez-Villanueva et al. (2023) found compensating trends — acceleration between the SAF and PF, deceleration on the southern flank of the PF — in passage (fixed-distance) coordinates; in streamwise coordinates the pattern is much reduced and the main significant trend is an SAF acceleration. Altimetric passage-coordinate trends have the same signs (2001–2025: SAF–PF zone +3.0 cm s⁻¹ per decade, p = 0.02; PF flank −0.4, p = 0.57). Main-text Fig. 2d uses bins between monthly-mean ADT streamlines (0.1 m wide in ψ), which smooth synoptic meanders; there two interfrontal bins accelerate significantly (+3.1, +5.2 cm s⁻¹ per decade) and two not (+2.7, +0.4); the SAF bins decelerate non-significantly (−1.1, −2.3), and three flank bins decelerate significantly (uncorrected; ψ = −0.25, −1.475, −1.625 m: −3.6, −1.3, −2.9). The altimetric result thus resembles their passage-coordinate result, not their streamwise SAF acceleration. Bin trends use annual means of at least six valid months.

**Surface integral Qₛ.** Qₛ sums the normal (positive eastward) monthly geostrophic velocity times the along-section spacing over the 121 points of the mean track. Since ug · n = (g/f) ∂η/∂s, at constant f Qₛ ∝ the endpoint ADT difference, so geographic, not ADT, endpoints are used. Months with ice-flag fraction > 0.10 at any point are excluded and annual means need three valid months: 235 of 300 months remain, 7–12 per year, but July–September in only 4–13 of 25 years, so Qₛ represents the ice-free season (Table S2). Qₛ is a kinematic surface diagnostic, not a volume transport; the barotropic component, roughly a quarter of the full-depth transport (Firing et al., 2011; Donohue et al., 2016), is invisible to it. Its nominal 2005–2019 increase rests on 15 annual values (9.5 % false-positive rate; Sect. S4) and on the period and seasonal sampling; it should not be equated with the in situ transport trend. The absence of a detectable 2001–2025 trend does not establish an unchanged full-depth transport.

## S3 Energetic pathway

This section supports the main-text statements that the half-power latitude has no significant trend for any parameter choice and that fixed ADT contours migrate poleward mainly with sea-level rise (Sects. 2.2, 3.1, 3.2 and 4).

**Detection and retention.** Following Chambers (2018), KE = ½|U|² of the monthly-mean geostrophic velocity on the 1/8° grid. At each longitude, a segment above the threshold (baseline 200 cm² s⁻², minimum width 100 km) defines an envelope extending from the peak to 25 % of the peak KE; the half-power latitude splits the trapezoidal KE integral into halves and so responds both to displacement and to unequal intensification of the flanks. Envelopes of the 2001–2025 mean KE define tracks; each month, the strongest envelope with half-power latitude inside a track's climatological segment is assigned to it. Of 4478 tracks, 2573 have their climatological half-power latitude in the ACC band. The circumpolar analysis keeps those matched in at least 90 % of months: 900 tracks (35 % of the ACC tracks) on 892 of the 2880 grid longitudes (31 %), 97 % mean coverage. Series are means over tracks of each track's anomaly.

**Ice mask.** The tracks (Fig. 1, Fig. 3b trend, Figs. S5–S7, Table S3) use KE with the permanent ice mask; recomputing KE with that mask reproduces them in 100 % of track-months (223 tracks, 75–50° W). The occurrence maps (Figs. 2b, 3b) count all monthly envelopes with half-power points in the band, from unmasked KE.

**Parameter sensitivity.** Thresholds of 150–400 cm² s⁻², a relative threshold of 20 % of each longitude's climatological maximum KE, envelope fractions of 20–50 % and minimum widths of 50–300 km give circumpolar trends of −1.9 to +1.8 km per decade, all with p ≥ 0.16 (baseline −0.5; Fig. S5a). Sector trends are within ±2.2 km per decade (p ≥ 0.37); none of the 33 bins of 5° reaches p < 0.05 (Fig. S5b). The retention rule favours persistent, possibly topographically steered jets; retention at 75 % (1301 tracks) or 50 % of months (1695) gives −0.8 [−4.1, +2.5] and −0.9 [−3.9, +2.0]; tracks over rough and smooth topography (split at the median standard deviation of depth in a ±2° box) give −1.0 [−4.7, +2.7] and −0.1 [−6.7, +6.6] km per decade. Fig. S6 compares products.

**Contour versus energy.** For each retained track and month we compute the half-power latitude, the latitude of the fixed ADT contour through the mean half-power point (closest crossing within ±3° at that longitude), and ADT (sea-level trend included) at the half-power point (Fig. S7, Table S3). With the median local meridional ADT gradient at the mean half-power latitudes (2.77 cm per 10 km, from the 2001–2025 mean ADT), the ADT rise implies a contour displacement of −9.5 km per decade, close to but smaller than the −11.5 measured. It moves south at 85 % of tracks and in every sector (−14.5, −12.7, −8.3 km per decade, Atlantic, Indian, Pacific; annual least squares, p < 0.01). For the 62 retained tracks in 70–55° W the same method gives 3.17 cm per 10 km and −8.4 km per decade, about two-thirds of the −12.8 measured. The contours pass through energy peaks, not SAF and PF levels.

**Synthetic detection experiments.** *Circumpolar:* the 2001–2025 mean KE on every fourth longitude (0.5°) is shifted northward along a linear ramp totalling 5, 10, 25 or 50 km over 2001–2025, plus observed KE anomalies (climatology and pixel-wise linear trend removed) resampled as whole fields in circular 36-month blocks,. Each of 41 realisations is fully reprocessed; a shift is detected when its Theil–Sen slope lies outside the central 95 % of the 41 no-shift slopes (not the test of Sect. S4). Detection rates are 12 % (5 km), 44 % (10 km) and 100 % (25 and 50 km); about 70 % of the shift is recovered (Fig. S5c, d). Scaled by this attenuation, the observed interval [−4.2, +3.1] km per decade corresponds to a bound on coherent displacement of about 6 km per decade. *Drake Passage:* the regional rule keeps envelopes in 70–55° W with climatological half-power south of 52° S (excluding the Malvinas Current), in the band, matched in at least 50 % of months (106 longitudes), with KE recomputed without the permanent mask. The climatological KE was shifted along a linear ramp of 0, 10 or 25 km plus observed monthly anomalies of resampled calendar years; 30 realisations per shift used the calibrated test. The 25 km shift was detected in 33 % (median recovered 21.7 km), 10 km in 27 %, the null in 3 %. This set differs from the 62 tracks of Table S3; its own trend is −6.8 [−21.9, +8.2] km per decade.

## S4 Trend statistics

This section supports the description of the trend test, its false-positive rates and the field-significance control (Sect. 2.5).

**Test.** The statistic is z = b/σ, with b the Theil–Sen slope of the deseasonalised series and σ the exact least-squares-slope standard deviation under an autoregressive (AR) model of the residuals, bias-corrected by bootstrap (Kilian, 1998) with the trend removal repeated, and shrunk to a root modulus of 0.99 if non-stationary. Nulls simulated from the corrected model are processed identically; p = (1 + #{|z_null| ≥ |z|})/(1 + N) and the interval is b ± q₉₅(|z_null|)σ, with N = 300 (smallest p 0.003) or 150 (Qₛ, 2° and 5° bins). Monthly series use the AIC order (≤ 12) and AR(12), reporting the larger p and the union of intervals; annual series use AR(1) with iterated bias correction.

**False-positive rates** at nominal 5 % (AIC order / AR(12)): AR(1) φ = 0.8, 5.3 % / 8 %; AR(12) fitted to Drake Passage EKE, 9.5 % / 3 %; AR(1) φ = 0.9, 3.5 % (AIC). The maximum rule is bounded by the smaller rate. Annual AR(1) (φ = 0.5): 5.0 % at n = 25, 9.5 % at n = 15. p near 0.05 carries ±0.01 Monte Carlo uncertainty.

**Maps.** Map nulls are pooled over 10 quantile classes of the bias-corrected AR coefficient sum (25 cells × 40 null series per class); a cell's p is the Gaussian tail of |z|/κ, κ = max(q₉₅/1.96, q₉₉/2.576) of the class null, the larger over the two noise models. False-discovery control at 10 % (Benjamini and Hochberg, 1995; Wilks, 2016) is applied over each map:

## S5 Isopycnal slope and wind sensitivity

This section supports the main-text statements on the σ₀ = 27.3 kg m⁻³ slope, its sector values and its field significance, and on the lagged correlations and regression coefficients (Sects. 2.4 and 3.3).

**Argo processing.** The Roemmich–Gilson fields (Roemmich and Gilson, 2009; 1°, 0–2000 dbar; 2004–2018 mean plus monthly anomalies of the 2019 release, extended to December 2025 with the provider's monthly files; 264 months) are converted each month to σ₀ with TEOS-10 (IOC, SCOR and IAPSO, 2010). The pressure p of σ₀ = 27.3 kg m⁻³ is interpolated linearly at the first subsurface crossing and is undefined where the surface outcrops or lies below 1975 dbar. The slope s = ∇p · n (dbar per 100 km; centred differences, periodic in longitude) uses n, the unit vector along the gradient of the 2001–2025 Product 1 mean ADT on the 1° grid, pointing equatorward across the streamlines; s > 0 where the isopycnal deepens equatorward, and along-stream gradients are excluded.

**Domain and trends.** The domain contains every 1° cell whose centre lies within 100 km (great circle) of a 1/8° pixel with mean ADT between −0.6 and +0.2 m, without any ice-masked pixel, and with s defined in all 22 annual fields and all 264 months: 2835 fixed cells, cos-latitude weighted. Percentages are relative to each series' time mean (all regional means positive; Table S4). In Fig. 4b, cell percentages use each cell's mean slope, negative in 68 cells; significance is tested on the absolute slope. Of the cells, 60 % have positive trends, 114 have p < 0.05 and 13, all positive, pass the false-discovery control; of 72 bins of 5°, 14 have p < 0.05 (all positive), none after control. Starting in 2007, when Argo sampling was denser, gives +1.3 % per decade (p = 0.010).

**Common domain.** Product 2 monthly eastward wind stress (0.25°) and Product 1 KE and EKE (main-text Eq. 1; ice pixels removed) are box-averaged onto the slope cells; cells with all fields defined in every month form the common domain (2830 cells).

**Lagged correlations (Fig. 4e).** Calendar-year means are detrended by least squares (wind over 2001–2025, responses over 2004–2025). At lags L = 0–3 yr, responses (2004–2025) are paired with the wind of years y − L (n = 22). The null (no relation with a wind series of the observed spectrum) uses 2000 phase-randomised surrogates of the detrended wind (Ebisuzaki, 1997), lagged alike. The grey envelope is the pointwise 95th percentile of surrogate |r|; the lag-search p compares each response's maximum |r| over the four lags with the surrogate maxima, accounting for the lag search but not for testing three responses.

**Regression coefficients (Fig. 4d).** Annual series are percentage anomalies from their 2004–2025 means, trends kept. The response in year y (2005–2025, n = 21) is regressed on the wind of year y − 1 by least squares with intercept, X′(y) = a + β τ′(y − 1). The lag (that of the correlation maximum) was not reselected in resampling; intervals are percentiles of 2000 bootstrap resamples of year pairs, ignoring serial dependence and lag selection; p tests r against 2000 phase-randomised surrogates of the annual wind (trend retained). For small anomalies β approximates a logarithmic sensitivity, d ln X/d ln τ. Because trends are kept, β mixes interannual covariability with trend covariance; the detrended interannual relation is the correlation; β is not an equilibrium sensitivity. The one-year lead is at the short end of reported lags: 2–3 years for the observed response to the 1998 wind maximum and 1.2–2.0 years (1.0–1.5 years for larger forcing) in the model of Meredith and Hogg (2006); annual means cannot resolve shorter lags.
