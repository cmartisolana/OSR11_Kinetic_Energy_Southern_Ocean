# Supporting information for Surface intensification of the Antarctic Circumpolar Current from 2001 to 2025

Martí-Solana et al., State of the Planet, Ocean State Report 11, Chapter 2

**Conventions.** Product 1: all-satellite SEALEVEL_GLO_PHY_L4_MY_008_047 (DT2024, 1/8°), used in the main text; Product 4: two-satellite SEALEVEL_GLO_PHY_CLIMATE_L4_MY_008_057 (1/4°); Product 3: the LM Gould ADCP record (Gutierrez-Villanueva et al., 2023). Unless stated, trends are Theil–Sen slopes of deseasonalised monthly series, 2001–2025, in percent of the period mean per decade, with 95 % intervals in brackets and p from the test of Sect. S4. Contrasts (trends of differences of mean-normalised series) are in percentage points ("points") per decade. Significance means p < 0.05; p = 0.050 is "at the threshold". In figures, open symbols mark p ≥ 0.05, including values that round to 0.050. Post hoc tests are labelled.

## S1 Two-satellite product

**Purpose.** Constellation changes over 2001–2025 may affect mapped EKE trends; a constant pair of altimeters shows which conclusions depend on the product.

**Method.** Product 4 diagnostics use the same definitions, regions, ice mask (ice fraction > 0.10 in any month), periods and test as Product 1. Grid spacing is 1/8° (Product 1) and 1/4° (Product 4); effective resolution is coarser than grid spacing and depends on mapping and sampling (Ballarotta et al., 2019; Product 1 QUID in main-text Table 1). Product differences are tested as the trend of the difference of mean-normalised series (Product 1 minus Product 4).

**Circumpolar results.** Over the ACC band (Table S1, Fig. S1) all trends have the same sign in both products. Mean-flow trends agree closely; EKE trends are about half as large in Product 4, mainly in the sub-monthly part, and diverge mainly after about 2018 (Fig. S1a), consistent with the time-varying constellation (Product 1 QUID; Hargous et al., 2026). No tested product difference is significant (p ≥ 0.09; p ≥ 0.12 on a common 1/4° grid). Product 4 relative KE and EKE trends are similar (+3.6 and +3.8 %); EKE − KE is not significant in either product. In the sectors, Pacific KE and EKE are significant in both products (p = 0.007–0.033), Indian EKE in Product 1 only (p = 0.010) and Atlantic EKE in neither (Product 1 p = 0.053); Product 4 KE is not significant in the Atlantic (p = 0.06) or Indian (p = 0.08) sector; along-stream speed trends agree between products. Over the regions of earlier studies (Fig. S1d), Product 1 EKE trends for 2001–2025 have the same sign as, and overlap within their intervals with, the trends of Hogg et al. (2015) and Martínez-Moreno et al. (2021) for earlier periods. [AUTHOR CHECK: Hogg et al. (2015) values and intervals]

**Energy-equivalent comparison.** A small relative speed trend corresponds approximately to twice that rate in squared speed: 4.0 versus 4.9 % per decade for monthly-mean KE in Product 1, and 3.6 versus 3.6 % in Product 4. This comparison is descriptive. EKE includes monthly velocity anomalies that also enter monthly-mean KE, so these diagnostics do not partition total kinetic energy into complementary mean and eddy components. Their trend contrast is neither an energy-transfer estimate nor a saturation test.

**Conclusion.** Signs and mean-flow trends are product-independent; the EKE magnitude differs by about a factor of two (not significantly), so statements resting on it, including the eddy–mean-flow contrast, depend on the product.

## S2 Drake Passage: comparison with the in situ record

**Purpose.** The main text uses altimetry alone. Here we check over 2005–2019 which altimetric quantities reproduce the variability measured from the ARSV Laurence M. Gould, in particular the cross-track velocity, and test the Drake Passage results that lie near the threshold or depend on mask, section or sampling choices.

**In situ record.** Product 3 comprises the ADCP transects of Gutierrez-Villanueva et al. (2023) across Drake Passage: cross-track velocity at 70–118 m, EKE integrated over 0–780 m and total transport over 0–760 m. The archived files hold 248 transects (February 2005–December 2019), the set Gutierrez-Villanueva et al. (2023) use for the record-length mean and net transport; their trend and EKE analyses use the 237 transects of October 2005–April 2019, the period adopted for every same-period comparison below. Total cross-track transport over 0–760 m (mean 75.5 Sv) = depth-independent reference part 51.1 + Ekman 8.1 + geostrophic relative to 760 m 15.9 Sv. EKE is the archive variable integrated over 0–780 m (calc_eke_uvprime.m, maxz = 780); Gutierrez-Villanueva et al. (2023) present it depth-averaged, a scaling that does not affect correlations.

**Comparison method.** Altimetric values from the monthly field of each transect's month are compared along the mean track, per transect and as annual means. As altimetry gives surface geostrophic flow and the in situ quantities include deeper and ageostrophic flow, the comparison checks variability, not magnitude. EKE and transport are compared north of about 58.5°S, ice-free in every month. The in situ band position is the latitude dividing the 0–760 m transport within the climatological altimetric envelope (55.1–59.3°S) into halves, so no shared variability is built in. Annual correlations are tested against surrogate series.

**Comparison results.** Over 2005–2019 Product 1 reproduces the mean cross-track velocity profile (r = 0.98; Fig. S2a), the velocity per 25 km bin and transect (r = 0.72 over 8187 pairs; anomalies r = 0.61; RMS difference 15 cm s⁻¹), EKE north of 58.5°S (r = 0.67 per transect, 0.89 for annual means) and surface transport north of 58.55°S against in situ transport over the same part of the section (r = 0.54 per transect, 0.67 annual; surrogate p = 0.003) (Tables S4 and S5; Fig. S2). The full-section in situ transport is not reproduced by surface transport over the northern section (r = 0.03 per transect and for annual means), as the in situ transports north and south of the ice-free limit vary in opposition (r = −0.54), nor by the whole-line integral including values mapped under seasonal ice (r = 0.07 per transect). This opposition is partly built into the split (a jet crossing a fixed latitude moves transport between the parts) and does not test the opposing frontal trends of Gutierrez-Villanueva et al. (2023). The half-power latitude of the merged energetic band along the section follows the in situ half-transport latitude with r = 0.50 per transect and 0.71 for annual means (0.50 with the profile truncated at the ice-free limit; Sect. S3), with a mean offset of 16 km and an RMS difference of 114 km, reflecting monthly fields compared with transects lasting about two days. Trends cannot be checked: over 15 annual values the in situ intervals (EKE −58.8 to +74.4 % per decade) are many times wider than the trends, and the overlap ends in 2019, as the products begin to diverge (Sect. S1). Over 2001–2025, EKE north of 58.5°S changes by +7.4 % per decade (−5.6 to +20.4; p = 0.24), not detectably.

**Changes along the transect (main-text Sect. 3.1).** The analysis follows the two coordinate systems of Gutierrez-Villanueva et al. (2023). In passage coordinates, the altimetric surface velocity is averaged in 25 km bins of distance from the north along the mean LM Gould track; the in situ velocity is the ADCP cross-track velocity at 70–118 m, with northbound transects reordered from the north and the ADCP offset applied as in their code. In streamwise coordinates, their streamline values ψ (Niiler–Maximenko mean dynamic topography minus 0.60 m plus sea-level anomaly) were mapped onto our ADT by sampling the monthly ADT of Product 1 at the mean position of each streamline pair on each transect (4005 pairs): ADT = 0.62 + 1.04ψ (r = 0.99; median offset 0.59 m, consistent with their 0.60 m). Zones: Subantarctic Front ψ = −0.40 to −0.60 m, zone between the fronts −0.60 to −1.00 m, Polar Front −1.00 to −1.25 m (their −1.00 to −1.30 m, limited by the bin edges of the archived files). Points with ice fraction above 0.10 are masked monthly; in streamwise coordinates a month is excluded for a zone when any of its points is ice-covered. Trends over October 2005–April 2019 use annual means for both records (AR(1) test); over 2001–2025, streamline-bin trends use annual means requiring at least six valid months, and fixed-coordinate series use monthly values where complete and annual means otherwise. Over 2005–2019 the bin-by-bin velocity-trend profiles of altimetry and in situ velocity correlate with r = 0.57 (Product 1) and 0.63 (Product 4); the 2001–2025 altimetric profiles correlate with the 2005–2019 in situ profile with r = 0.64 and 0.62 (Fig. S3). The zone trends (Table S6) reproduce the sign pattern of Gutierrez-Villanueva et al. (2023) in passage coordinates: acceleration between the SAF and PF, deceleration on the southern flank of the PF and acceleration at the SACCF. In streamwise coordinates neither record shows a significant transport trend with this test, and the significant Polar Front EKE decrease reported by Gutierrez-Villanueva et al. (2023, their Fig. 7c) is not seen in altimetry. The southern zones (PF flank, SACCF) are seasonally ice-covered, so their altimetric trends rest on ice-free months only.

**Net surface transport across the fixed line (main-text Sect. 2.3).** The surface integral sums normal geostrophic velocity times along-section spacing at the 121 points of the mean track. ## S3 Half-power method: parameters, detectability and the Drake Passage section

**Purpose.** A bound on the displacement of the energetic band is meaningful only if the detection parameters do not drive it and the detectable shift is known.

**Method.** Following Chambers (2018), on monthly-mean KE: at each longitude, envelopes extend from peaks above a KE threshold to where KE falls to a fraction of the peak (minimum width imposed); the half-power latitude is the median of KE integrated across the envelope. Baseline: 200 cm² s⁻² (the rounded median, 195 cm² s⁻², of the 2001–2025 mean KE over the ACC band), 25 %, 100 km. As a KE-weighted median it also responds to unequal intensification of the flanks, so it bounds the displacement of the energy distribution, not of a jet axis. Monthly envelopes are matched to the climatological envelope; longitudes matched in ≥ 90 % of months are kept (900, about 31 % of the band). Sensitivity: thresholds 150–400 cm² s⁻², fractions 20–50 %, widths 50–300 km. Detectability: shifts imposed on climatological profiles plus observed anomalies.

**Results.** Every parameter set gives p ≥ 0.16 for the circumpolar trend (Table S8, Fig. S5a); none of the 33 bins of 5° reaches p < 0.05 (Fig. S5b). Over the regions of earlier studies, trends are small and not significant (km per decade, 95 % intervals): 90–170°E −1.6 (−12.9 to +9.6) and 160–90°W +5.6 (−4.9 to +16.2), where Chambers (2018) found −29 and +46 over 1993–2016; 75–150°E −1.5 (−9.4 to +6.5), where Kim and Orsi (2014) reported a southward drift over 1992–2011; 150–80°W (Pacific–Antarctic Ridge meander) +5.9 (−5.3 to +17.1), against about +3 over 1993–2023 (Liu et al., 2026). Over 25 years, a 25 km shift is detected in 100 % of realisations and a 10 km shift in 44 %; about 70 % of a shift is recovered (Fig. S5c, d). Detection frequency is 25–45 % at about 170–150°W and 115–75°W (including the Drake upstream region), which are excluded. The unmatched median latitude of the discussion version (at most 27 % power for a 50 km shift; −0.002 to −0.007° yr⁻¹ from amplitude growth alone) was replaced. Three-year blocks (8 values, OLS as in Chambers, 2018): ACC p = 0.17; Indian sector p = 0.049, a single borderline value. The half-power trends of both products agree (Fig. S6).

## S4 Trend-test calibration

**Purpose.** Monthly series are strongly autocorrelated, and common trend tests then reject too often.

**Method.** The statistic is the Theil–Sen slope over the exact OLS-slope standard deviation under a fitted autoregressive (AR) noise model, bias-corrected by bootstrap including trend removal, with coefficient correction adapted from Kilian (1998); non-stationary fits are shrunk to a maximum root of 0.99. p and the 95 % interval are calibrated against 300 null series processed identically (smallest p 0.0033, reported as 0.003). Monthly series use AIC-selected and AR(12) orders, reporting the larger p and the union of intervals; the two need not agree exactly at the threshold (Drake box EKE: interval excludes zero, p = 0.050). Annual series use AR(1) with iterated bias correction. For 1° maps the calibration is pooled over 10 persistence classes (p from |z|/κ, κ = max(q95/1.96, q99/2.576) of the class null). The false discovery rate (FDR) is controlled at 10 %, following Wilks (2016; α_FDR = 2α_global for correlated fields).

**Results.** AIC is liberal for the Drake-like AR(12) process (dropping small high-lag coefficients) and AR(12) for pure AR(1); the maximum rule takes the more conservative (Table S11). Fifteen annual values cannot be tested at the nominal level (9.5 %); no in situ trend is significant under either test. With 300 null series, p near 0.05 has a Monte Carlo uncertainty of about ±0.01, so p of about 0.03–0.07 is marginal. Rejected alternatives: moving-block bootstrap 13–18 %; uncalibrated AR sieve 7–13 %; Hamed–Rao 25–33 %. An independent re-implementation matched the analytic slope standard deviation to Monte Carlo within 0.3 %.

**Conclusion.** Monthly trends with p ≤ 0.02 are unlikely to be artefacts of autocorrelation; p of about 0.03–0.07 is marginal; 15 annual values cannot be tested at the nominal level.

## S5 Argo isopycnal slope (main-text Fig. 4)

The Roemmich–Gilson 2019-release temperature and salinity climatology and monthly anomalies are extended through 2025 using the provider's monthly files (Roemmich and Gilson, 2009). Fields are on a 1° grid over approximately 0–2000 dbar. Annual means are converted to Absolute Salinity and Conservative Temperature with the Gibbs SeaWater toolbox; σ₀ is calculated using TEOS-10 (IOC, SCOR and IAPSO, 2010). The pressure of σ₀ = 27.3 kg m⁻³ is found by linear interpolation at its first subsurface crossing; cells where the surface outcrops or lies below 1975 dbar are excluded. Pressure is used as an approximate depth coordinate.

**Slope.** The signed cross-stream slope is s = ∇p·n̂, with n̂ the unit vector along the gradient of the 2001–2025 mean ADT of Product 1 (averaged onto the 1° grid), pointing equatorward across the streamlines; s is in dbar per 100 km and is positive where the isopycnal deepens equatorward. Gradients are centred differences on the 1° grid, periodic in longitude. Unlike the gradient magnitude, s excludes along-stream gradients and is the component that enters the thermal-wind shear across the current.

**Domain.** The ACC band of the main text (mean ADT −0.6 to +0.2 m) widened by 100 km: every 1° cell whose centre lies within 100 km (great circle) of a 1/8° band pixel. Cells containing any sea-ice-masked altimetry pixel are removed, and a cell is kept only if s is defined in every month of 2004–2025 (264 months), so every regional mean uses a fixed set of 2835 cells.

**Trends.** The slope is computed each month from the monthly temperature and salinity fields. Regional series (domain, sectors, 5° longitude bins; cos-latitude weights) use the calibrated Theil–Sen test of Sect. S4 on the deseasonalised monthly series (larger p of the AIC-order and AR(12) noise models). Maps use the same statistic per cell, calibrated per persistence class (10 classes of the bias-corrected AR coefficient sum, 25 cells × 40 null series each, for each noise model), with Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery control at 10 % over the domain.

**Results.** The domain-mean slope steepens by +1.1 dbar per 100 km per decade (+0.3 to +1.9; +1.2 % per decade, p = 0.02). The annual means of the monthly series give the same trend (+1.2 % per decade, p = 0.017) and correlate with the slope computed from annual-mean fields at r = 0.98 (that version: +1.4 % per decade, p = 0.003, 2951 cells). The steepening is significant in the Atlantic sector (+2.2 % per decade, p = 0.013) and not in the Pacific (+1.5 %, p = 0.24) or Indian (+0.2 %, p = 0.58) sectors. Of the 2835 cells, 60 % have positive trends; 114 have p < 0.05 and 13 pass the false-discovery control, all positive. Of the 72 bins of 5° longitude, 14 have p < 0.05 (all positive) and none passes the false-discovery control. Across bins, the slope trend correlates with the EKE trend of Product 1 over the same cells and years (r = +0.43; p = 0.03 from circular shifts of the profile; main-text Fig. 4d), and the EKE trend correlates with the wind-stress trend (r = +0.54, p = 0.07). With Theil–Sen trends of all three variables over the common period 2004–2025 on the same bins (main-text Fig. 4d), r = +0.54 (wind–EKE, p = 0.08), +0.25 (wind–slope, p = 0.39) and +0.47 (EKE–slope, p = 0.03); time-mean values (|r| ≤ 0.35, p ≥ 0.13) and pooled interannual anomalies (|r| ≤ 0.08) correlate less; the detrended annual series do not correlate (|r| ≤ 0.40 for lags of −2 to +2 yr, family-wise p = 0.27). In Drake Passage (2° bins, 85–35°W, of the band between the mean SAF and PF widened by 100 km; not shown) the slope trends lie between −2.2 and +5.1 % per decade, three bins (43–39°W) have p < 0.05, and they do not follow the EKE trends (r = −0.01, p = 0.96).

**Wind stress.** These tests use the annual slope (2951 cells). Eastward wind stress (Product 2) averaged over the same cells increases by +2.2 % per decade over 2001–2025 (+0.2 to +4.2; p = 0.03, annual test; +2.4 %, p = 0.09 with the monthly test) and by +2.2 % per decade over 2004–2025 (p = 0.09). The slope is not related to the wind: the detrended annual slope does not correlate with the wind stress at lags of 0–3 yr (wind leading; |r| ≤ 0.30, family-wise p = 0.52; 0.32 and p = 0.78 for the 45–60°S zonal band), and across 5° bins the slope and wind trends do not correlate (r = +0.21, p = 0.49; r = +0.20, p = 0.36 with the monthly-test slope trends of main-text Fig. 4d).

**Response to the wind.** Over the cells common to all variables (2830 cells; 2004–2025), eastward wind stress increases by +2.4 % per decade (−0.8 to +5.5, p = 0.15), KE (0.5|U|² of the monthly velocity) by +5.2 % (p = 0.003), EKE by +8.9 % (p = 0.017) and the slope by +1.2 % (p = 0.02). The ratios to the wind trend (not shown), with 95 % intervals from a joint moving-block residual bootstrap (24-month blocks), are 2.2 (1.1–8.7) for KE, 3.8 (1.7–15.2) for EKE and 0.5 (0.2–2.4) for the slope; the wide upper bounds reflect the uncertain wind trend. In the detrended annual series, KE and EKE follow the wind with a lag of one year (main-text Fig. 4e; r = +0.61 and +0.57; family-wise p over lags 0–3 yr = 0.004 and 0.008, from phase-randomised wind surrogates), whereas the slope does not (r ≤ 0.24, family-wise p = 0.71). Along the ACC (72 bins of 5°), the lag-1 correlation of EKE with the wind is positive in 58 bins (median r = +0.20; 9 bins with p < 0.05, none after false-discovery control) and that of the slope in 44 bins (median r = +0.10; 5 bins with p < 0.05, none after false-discovery control). Pooling the standardised, detrended annual anomalies of all bins (1512 points; not shown), EKE correlates with the wind stress of the previous year (r = +0.21, p < 0.001, from phase-randomised wind surrogates with the same phases in all bins) and the slope does so weakly (r = +0.09, p = 0.07). Keeping the trends, the circumpolar annual anomalies of year y against the wind stress of year y − 1 (2005–2025; main-text Fig. 4d) give sensitivities d ln X/d ln τ of +0.49 % per % for KE (+0.06 to +0.79), +0.91 for EKE (+0.20 to +1.49) and +0.09 for the slope (−0.01 to +0.22; p = 0.19). Along the ACC (not shown) the EKE elasticity is positive in 64 of 72 bins (median +0.37 % per %; 14 bins with p < 0.05, none after false-discovery control) and the slope elasticity is near zero (median +0.04; 50 bins positive, 9 with p < 0.05, 5 after false-discovery control).

These Argo fields are spatially smoothed and early Argo sampling (before about 2007) is sparse, which limits interpretation of narrow fronts and of single cells; the regional results are stable when the record starts in 2007.
