# Outline v3.2 — agreed 19 Oct 2026

**Central question.** Has the ACC responded to stronger westerlies as eddy saturation predicts (more eddy energy, little change in transport and pathway)? Surface view, 2001–2025, altimetry only.

**Logic.** Results follow the surface predictions of eddy saturation: forcing → eddies → mean flow and transport → pathway → mechanism at the meanders. Drake Passage first (Referees A, C), then the whole ACC. Pathway after the eddies, with a forward reference in 3.2 (agreed).

## 1 Introduction (~600)
1. Context: westerlies/SAM; why the ACC response matters.
2. Theory: coarse models accelerate (Hall and Visbeck 2002; Saenko et al. 2005); eddy saturation (Straub 1993; Hallberg and Gnanadesikan 2001, 2006; Munday et al. 2013; Constantinou and Hogg 2019; Maddison et al. 2025); mechanisms: baroclinic eddies; barotropic wakes (Tansley and Marshall 2001); full standing-meander chain (Thompson and Naveira Garabato 2014).
3. Evidence by component: eddies (Meredith and Hogg 2006; Hogg et al. 2015; Zhang et al. 2021; Martínez-Moreno et al. 2021); transport (Böning et al. 2008; Koenig et al. 2014; Donohue et al. 2016; Gutierrez-Villanueva et al. 2023 — no net trend, opposing frontal trends in passage coordinates that largely vanish in streamwise coordinates except SAF acceleration, EKE up on the SAF south flank and down in the PF; Hogg et al. 2015 slope index); pathway (Sokolov and Rintoul 2009b; Kim and Orsi 2014; Gille 2014; Chambers 2018; Schiavon et al. 2025 = CMIP6 projections: jets anchored by topography); meanders (Thompson and Naveira Garabato 2014; Liu et al. 2026).
4. Gap and contribution (i–iv); OSR update framing.
