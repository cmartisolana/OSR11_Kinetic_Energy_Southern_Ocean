## 1 Take-home message and titles

**One sentence.** Altimetry, which reproduces the interannual variability of velocity and EKE measured from ships in Drake Passage, shows that over 2001–2025 the ACC's surface eddy field and mean flow strengthened together along a pathway with no detectable displacement, while surface transport proxies changed several times less, a pattern consistent with, but not a test of, eddy saturation.

**Titles** (keep "eddy saturation" out of them):
- A (recommended): *Southern Ocean surface kinetic energy trends, 2001–2025: an altimetric update anchored in Drake Passage observations*
- B: *Stronger eddies and mean flow along an undisplaced Antarctic Circumpolar Current pathway, 2001–2025*
- C: *From Drake Passage to the circumpolar band: updated surface kinetic energy and transport-proxy trends of the ACC*

## 2 Novelty statement

**Framing.** The OSR updates earlier evidence, and an update is informative whatever its outcome. The novelty rests on four points, not on the extra years:

1. **In situ anchoring at matched coverage (relative to GV23).** Over 2005–2019, altimetry reproduces the interannual variability of:
   - EKE north of 58.5°S (annual r = 0.89);
   - velocity (r = 0.72, 8,187 pairs);
   - transport north of 58.5°S (annual r = 0.67);
   - band position (r = 0.71).

   It does **not** reproduce full-section transport (r ≈ 0.05); the in situ north and south parts vary in opposition (r = −0.55). This defines what altimetry can extend.
2. **A matched, conservative update (relative to MM21 and Hogg).** The comparison uses one autocorrelation-calibrated test, absolute and relative trends, and two products.
   - 35–65°S EKE: +4.6 % per decade over 2001–2020 (p = 0.11) and +6.6 % [1.5, 11.6] over 2001–2025 (p = 0.03), against MM21's +5.1 % (1993–2020).
   - Hogg Indian and Pacific boxes: all-sat +9.0 and +8.6 %, two-sat +4.8 and +4.9 %. These bracket Hogg's +6.0 and +6.4 %, which is consistency, not reproduction.
3. **The mean flow examined with the eddies.**
   - Along-stream speed rises +2.0 % per decade (p = 0.003).
   - The excess of EKE over KE is not significant (+3.0 %, −0.1 to +6.1, p = 0.060).
   - Tracked eddies become stronger (amplitude +4.6 %, rotational speed +3.8 %) but not more numerous or larger.
4. **A pathway bound with stated detectability, and the surface ingredients of Thompson and Naveira Garabato (2014).**
   - Position: −0.5 km per decade (−4.2 to +3.1); a 25 km shift would have been detected.
   - The jet accelerates in 51 of 70 bins.
   - Sinuosity and latitude show no tendency.
   - There is no concentration of the EKE increase in the wakes (+0.1 % per decade, p = 0.95).

**Do not claim:**
- "first";
- a test of frontal compensation;
- any Hogg-index result (still [PENDING]).

## 4 Narrative arc and transitions

Binding decision 3 fixes the order a–d, so the check comes third; the end of Sect. 2 must state that Sect. 3.3 licenses the extension. Moving (c) first would make the logic linear (author's decision).

Budget: Introduction 600, Data and methods 1300, Results 2100, Discussion 1000 words.

**Transitions** (what each metric tests; which outcome would be consistent with saturation):
- **Introduction → 3.1:** Under eddy saturation, stronger forcing should raise eddy energy more than the mean flow and transport, and Drake Passage is the one place with an independent record, so we start with wind, KE and EKE in, upstream and downstream of the passage.
  - Result: box wind +8.7 %. In the passage, EKE rises +8.4 % (p = 0.050, all-sat only) while KE and speed are flat. Upstream, KE, EKE and wind all rise.
- **3.1 → 3.2:** A box EKE change could reflect a jet moving into or out of the box, so we ask whether the energetic band along the section has moved; a detectable shift would make the box trends ambiguous.
  - Result: −10 km per decade [−36, +16], a weak bound.
- **3.2 → 3.3:** Altimetric changes can be carried beyond 2019 and beyond the section only for quantities whose ship-measured variability altimetry reproduces; because surface geostrophic flow and 0–760 m differ, agreement concerns variability, not magnitude.
- **3.3 → 3.4:** Because only the part north of 58.5°S is reproduced, transport is treated as three distinct quantities (northern surface proxy, in situ 0–760 m total, model-calibrated slope index [PENDING]). A transport increase comparable to the EKE increase would be hard to reconcile with saturation, while no detectable trend is consistent with it without testing it.
- **3.4 → 3.5:** The clearest regional change lies upstream, outside the section, and velocity and EKE variability are reproduced, so we extend these quantities around the ACC and compare them with MM21 and Hogg, separating period, product and test from changes in the ocean.
- **3.5 → 3.6:** Saturation concerns how the added energy is partitioned, so EKE is set against along-stream speed and the surface transport proxy; an EKE trend clearly exceeding the mean-flow trend would be consistent with saturation, while comparable trends would indicate a joint intensification that surface data cannot attribute.
- **3.6 → 3.7:** A fixed-band trend could reflect a moving pathway, and the standing-meander mechanism predicts upstream acceleration, flexing and EKE localised downstream of topography, so we ask which of these surface ingredients is detectable.
- **Discussion:** same order, then what remains open and the limits.

**Overstatements in v23**
1. "meander curvature shows no trend at any longitude": the quantity is sinuosity (distinct from curvature per the replies; Sect. 2.4 contradicts this), and 3 bins have p < 0.05.
2. "EKE grows most where the jet accelerates most": write "larger where larger" (r = 0.40 across bins).
3. "The two [Drake proxies] are uncorrelated … little interannual skill": superseded by the matched result (r = 0.67).
4. "Neither … shows a trend": write "no detectable trend".
5. "neither captures … barotropic contributions": the in situ total includes the depth-independent part (51.1 of 75.5 Sv). What it misses is the transport below 760 m.
6. "establishes … intensification of the jets in place": write "consistent with". Asymmetric intensification alone moves the half-power position by 10 km (G2).
7. "addresses the mechanism directly" and "the meanders neither flex nor move": the analysis covers surface ingredients only, and a 25 km flexing was not detectable.
8. "the magnitude of the EKE increase is not [robust]": the product difference is not significant (+4.3, −2.2 to +10.8, p = 0.12).
9. Drake "EKE − KE … significant (p = 0.04)": this holds in all-sat only (two-sat p = 0.22) and depends on the mask.
10. "[Chambers' shifts] belonged to the earlier period" and "transport responds to the circumpolar wind stress": hypotheses; hedge.
11. "eddy saturation at the surface": saturation concerns depth-integrated transport.
12. "five times more slowly": give about 5 (all-sat) and about 2.5 (two-sat), and say the ratio is not a saturation test.
13. "We test this": change to "We examine whether … are consistent with".
14. "SWOT … resolve the subsurface steps": it cannot.
15. Check that Schiavon et al. (2025) supports "anchoring in climate projections".

## 7 Risks a sceptical referee will still raise

1. **"Not new."** Put §2 at the end of the Introduction; cite MM21 and Hogg throughout.
2. **"Which EKE trend is right?"** Give both products in the abstract. The 2005–2019 in situ EKE trend (+7.8 %) is closer to all-sat (+10.1 %) than to two-sat (+0.8 %), but all three are non-significant, so this is a hint only.
3. **"The check misses the critical period."** The in situ overlap ends in 2019, and the products diverge after about 2018. Say so: the check concerns variability, not trends or the later constellation.
4. **"Smaller transport trend than EKE ≠ saturation."** The circumpolar wind-stress trend is not significant (+2.5 %, p = 0.08), so flat transport cannot be read as insensitivity to wind. Put this in the first paragraph of the Discussion.
5. **"The mean flow strengthens."** Speed +2.0 %, circumpolar proxy +1.6 %. Do not hide it; the surface data do not discriminate, so leave it open.
6. **"The mechanism is untested."** State what is observable: upstream acceleration (upstream KE robust, speed borderline; PAR core +0.019 m s⁻¹ per decade, p = 0.01, upstream p = 0.06); flexing not detectable; localisation not found. The upstream-Drake box lies in the Pacific, where speed rises sector-wide (+3.5 %), so the signal may not be meander-specific.
7. **Borderline p-values and many tests.** Base no conclusion on results with p ≈ 0.05 (Drake EKE, upstream speed, Hogg Pacific two-sat). Name the pre-specified tests and flag the post hoc 400 cm² s⁻² run.
8. **EKE level about twice MM21's** (1.44 vs about 0.72 PJ m⁻¹), not investigated. Explain it or report candidate causes.
9. **The Hogg index may be dominated by sea ice.** The smoke test, which is not a result, found 47 % of slope points ice-free. Write Sect. 3.4 so that it holds for any outcome, and label the conversion as model-calibrated.
10. **Referee C's own data.** Define the 0–760 m total precisely; reconcile transect counts (244 vs 248).
