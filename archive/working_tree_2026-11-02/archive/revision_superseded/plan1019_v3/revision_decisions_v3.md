Reviews: review_PO.md (physical oceanographer), review_HI.md (impact / Referee C role), numbercheck_v3.md. Apply everything below; where a review item is not listed here, use judgement consistent with brief_v3.md and binding_decisions.md. Keep the authors' cautious voice; do not add claims.

## A. Framing change (most important)
1. **Energy vs velocity (PO M2; HI M2).** EKE and KE scale with velocity squared; the along-stream speed and the surface transport proxies scale with velocity. Remove "five times", "several times less", "transport proxies change several times less" everywhere (abstract, 3.4 question, 3.6, 4, summary). In 3.6 state: the ratio of relative trends is 5.0 (all-sat) and 2.5 (two-sat); in energy-equivalent terms (twice the relative trend of a velocity-like quantity) the circumpolar proxy rises at about 40 % of the EKE rate in the all-satellite product (3.2 vs 8.0) and at a comparable rate in the two-satellite product (3.0 vs 3.8), and the along-stream speed (4.0) at about the KE rate (4.9); in Drake Passage the northern proxy (energy-equivalent 7.8) is comparable with box EKE (8.4). This describes surface partition and is not a saturation test.
2. **Consequent conclusion.** The surface record shows a joint intensification of eddies and mean flow; the eddy excess over the mean flow is not significant (EKE − KE +3.0 percentage points per decade, p = 0.060) and the eddy–transport contrast is modest and product-dependent in like terms. Conclusion wording (abstract end, Sect. 4 summary): the observations are "compatible with eddy saturation but do not discriminate it from a joint intensification of the surface flow", because (i) the circumpolar wind-stress trend is not detected, (ii) the contrast is small in like terms and product-dependent, (iii) the full-depth transport is unobserved, (iv) equilibrium cannot be separated from slow adjustment. Keep "consistent with" only where literally true. Add HI M3/PO S3: the circumpolar EKE trend (8.0 %) exceeds the undetected stress trend (2.5 %), so the EKE increase cannot be attributed to the circumpolar wind on this evidence either (cite Marshall et al., 2017 [verify] and Maddison et al., 2025 for EKE ∝ stress scaling under saturation); SAM not significantly correlated (S10).
3. **Licensing (PO M4).** The Drake comparison licenses interannual variability at that section; extension elsewhere is by plausibility, not test. Fix 3.3 question, 4 ¶1 and the summary ("Altimetry reproduces … across Drake Passage. Around the ACC, the altimetric record shows …").
4. **Novelty/gap (PO M1 + HI M1).** GV23 already found an in situ EKE increase between SAF and PF consistent with altimetric EKE trends and with eddy saturation; their "compensation" means opposing trends among frontal regions (not interannual anticorrelation). Hogg et al. (2015) paired EKE with the slope transport index; Chambers (2018) and Sokolov and Rintoul (2009b) studied the pathway. New gap sentence: "To our knowledge, altimetric and in situ variability have not been compared at matched coverage to define what the altimetric record can extend, and eddy, mean-flow, transport and pathway changes have been estimated in separate studies with different periods, products and tests." Adjust 3.3/4 statements about compensation accordingly (the north–south opposition of interannual variability "echoes" nothing specific; say it is partly by construction and does not test GV23's frontal compensation).
5. **Title (HI S7):** "Surface eddy and mean-flow trends of the Antarctic Circumpolar Current, 2001–2025, from altimetry compared with Drake Passage observations". Same title on the SI.
